## How attribution was determined

Commit author metadata, parent relationships, changed-file lists, and selected diffs were reviewed. Each commit appears exactly once in the individual ledger below. A merge is credited as integration work to its author; the implementation it imports remains credited to its original commit authors. Snapshot copies, moves, and repeated merges do not establish new ownership of the copied implementation.

The work descriptions summarize changes recorded in history; they do not certify that every historical feature worked correctly. The original commit subject is retained so that descriptions such as "Small fixes" can be checked against the linked diff. Dates are author dates in the recorded offsets, which are +03:00 or +02:00 in this history.

Git commits do not record the branch on which they were originally created. Current branch containment is listed exactly, and historical source branch names come from merge subjects. Deleted branches and unreachable commits cannot be exhaustively reconstructed from this history. No co-author trailers were found; uncommitted, pair-programming, review, or offline work is not attributable from these records.
